== Risks and Technical Debts

ifdef::arc42help[]
[role="arc42help"]
****
.Contents
A list of identified technical risks or technical debts, ordered by priority

.Motivation
“Risk management is project management for grown-ups” (Tim Lister, Atlantic Systems Guild.) 

This should be your motto for systematic detection and evaluation of risks and technical debts in the architecture, which will be needed by management stakeholders (e.g. project managers, product owners) as part of the overall risk analysis and measurement planning.

.Form
List of risks and/or technical debts, probably including suggested measures to minimize, mitigate or avoid risks or reduce technical debts.

The table below lists the most relevant technical risks and accepted
technical debts of *Drop*, ordered by impact. They are reviewed every
release cycle.

=== Risks

[cols="1,2,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| ID | Risk | Impact | Mitigation

| R1
| Vendor lock-in to Firebase / GCP (ADR-4, ADR-5)
| A change of cloud provider or messaging vendor would require
re-platforming of authentication, real-time data and push.
| Encapsulate Firebase usage behind narrow ports in the backend; keep
the option to swap Firestore for another real-time database without
touching the bounded contexts.

| R2
| Vendor lock-in to Stripe Connect (ADR-3)
| If Stripe changes terms or becomes unavailable for the region,
charges, payouts and commission split are blocked.
| Isolate Stripe in the dedicated Payment Service; integration goes
through a single adapter so a second processor can be plugged in.

| R3
| Market saturation and competition from established ride-sharing
providers
| Slow user adoption may prevent reaching the volume that justifies
extracting microservices from the monolith.
| Modular monolith (ADR-2) keeps operational cost low while user base
grows; extraction is deferred until load justifies it.

| R4
| Regulatory changes for ride-sharing in operating regions
| Local regulations may restrict operation or require new compliance
features at short notice.
| Keep regulatory rules (driver verification, fare caps, reporting)
behind dedicated services / configuration so they can be adjusted
per region without touching core flows.

| R5
| GDPR / DSGVO non-compliance (constraint OC1)
| Fines and reputational damage; possible suspension of operation in
the EU.
| Data minimisation, EU-region data residency, account-deletion flow,
regular privacy review (chapter 8).

| R6
| Battery and data consumption of live tracking on driver devices
| Drivers may disable the app or uninstall it, breaking the matching
process.
| Tune the update frequency of the Realtime Service; fall back to
lower-frequency updates on slow networks.

| R7
| External map / routing provider outage or rate-limit hit
| Search and booking degrade for all users.
| Circuit Breaker + Retry-with-Backoff (chapter 8); short-term cache
of recent routes; fail fast with RFC 7807 error so the app can show
a clear message.

| R8
| Driver-verification provider unavailable
| New drivers cannot be onboarded.
| Verification can fall back to a manual in-house process (constraint
OC6 / chapter 3) so onboarding continues.
|===

=== Accepted Technical Debts

[cols="1,2,3", options="header"]
|===
| ID | Debt | Plan

| D1
| Monolith deploys the Analytics context together with the operational
contexts.
| Acceptable until reporting load impacts operational latency; extract
Analytics first when that happens (the seam is already in place,
chapter 5).

| D2
| No multi-region active/active deployment.
| Sufficient for the initial Austrian rollout. Revisit when the first
country outside the EU is added.

| D3
| Manual approval step between `staging` and `prod` in CI/CD.
| Kept on purpose while the team is small to avoid unattended bad
deploys; revisit once automated end-to-end coverage is high enough to
trust full continuous deployment.
|===
